# 0017. Phone play: landscape flight, no page zoom, clear thumb zones

- Status: Implemented in 4.4a and 4.4b; amended in 4.4c (phone HUD) after the
  owner's first iPhone check; checks on the owner's phones follow
  deployment.
- Date: 2026-11-07

## Context

Step 4.3 gave the game touch controls. On 2026-11-07 the owner decided that
flight is landscape-only with a rotate prompt, that pinch zoom and double-tap
zoom are blocked, and that the test devices are an iPhone 15 Pro and a Samsung
Galaxy A70. Phones are tested on the deployed site after merging, so the layout
has to be right in emulation and easy to diagnose on device.

## Decision

- **Landscape flight, any-orientation menus.** A phone or tablet is a device
  whose primary pointer is coarse and cannot hover. In portrait, while a flight is
  under way (flying or paused), the game sets the HUD's `rotatePrompt` and pauses.
  The prompt covers the scene below the header, so language, sound, and Help stay
  usable. Turning back hides it and leaves the flight paused, as after a hidden
  tab: the player resumes with the thumbs in place. The start card, Help, and the
  results card work upright. No Screen Orientation lock: browsers allow it only
  in fullscreen, and an installed app declares landscape in its manifest (4.4b).
- **No page zoom, three layers.** The viewport meta tag sets `maximum-scale=1`
  and `user-scalable=no` (honoured by Android Chrome). CSS gives the page
  `touch-action: manipulation` (no double-tap zoom) and the game surface
  `touch-action: none` (no pan or pinch), with no overscroll, selection, or
  callout. iOS Safari ignores `user-scalable=no` for accessibility, so `zoom.ts`
  cancels its proprietary `gesturestart/change/end` events and the second tap of
  a double tap outside interactive elements, only when the event is cancelable.
  The 3D view's own pinch (camera zoom) uses pointer events and keeps working;
  Help and the results card still scroll inside themselves.
- **Clear thumb zones.** When the touch controls show, the HUD reflects
  `touch-controls`: the readouts become one strip along the top left, the radar
  and pad distance sit between the thumbs at the bottom, and the throttle gauge
  gives way to the lever, which already shows throttle and hover. The safe-sink
  scale and fuel bar give way too (the amber caution on vertical speed remains).
- **Edge to edge.** `viewport-fit=cover`, and every layer offsets by
  `env(safe-area-inset-*)`: the notch side in landscape and the home indicator.
- **Short screens without touch** (a short laptop window, or a phone with touch
  controls set to Never): the start card shows without the instruments behind it,
  and the results card puts its checks in a second column.

- **Installable, not offline** (4.4b). A web app manifest, generated by the Vite
  config from the theme (name, colours, the 4.2c icons including the maskable
  one, `start_url` and `scope` relative to the manifest, so `/gravitas/` on
  Pages), declares `orientation: landscape` and `display: standalone` with
  `display_override: ["fullscreen", "standalone"]`: Android Chrome opens it full
  screen; iOS, which reads only `display`, standalone. iOS also gets
  `apple-mobile-web-app-*` tags for its title and a translucent status bar.
  No service worker: offline play and update prompts remain a later decision.

## Amendment (4.4c): corner chips on phones

The owner tried v0.3.0 on an iPhone 15 Pro in Safari. With the toolbar showing,
the page is 734 × 331: the readout strip wrapped to two rows and covered about a
quarter of the screen, the radar sat bottom centre exactly over the pad, the yaw
buttons were oversized, and upright the "How to fly" text ran off the right edge
over the full desktop instrument panel. After a verdict the HUD switched back to
the desktop panels. The emulation had used the full 852 × 393 screen. On
2026-11-07 the owner chose design option A, "corner chips":

- **A clear centre.** On a phone in landscape (`max-height: 500px`) with the touch
  layout, nothing sits in the middle third of the width below the top corner
  chips, where the chase camera keeps the lander and the opening faces the pad.
  One readout card top left (it never wraps); pause and ⋯ (40 px) top right; an
  86 px radar card under them with the pad distance; the 40 px yaw buttons in a
  column left of it; a slim lever on the left edge below the card; the stick zone
  bottom right, starting below the radar. The phase line and Start sit in the top
  band between the corners.
- **One layout in every phase.** The HUD's new `touchLayout` (attribute
  `touch-layout`) says the player uses the touch controls, in every phase; the
  game sets it from the touch-controls setting and the device in use.
  `touchControls` still says whether they show now. Ready and complete keep the
  corner layout; the start card hides the readouts behind it as before.
- **A ⋯ menu.** On phones, language, sound, restart, and Help move into a native
  popover (`popover`/`popovertarget`): Escape or a tap outside closes it and
  focus returns to ⋯; opening it mid-flight pauses, like Help. Help opened from
  it returns focus to ⋯.
- **Upright start screen.** In portrait (`max-width: 500px`) with the touch
  layout: a slim header (mark and name, ⋯), "How to fly" with wrapping text, a
  "turn your phone to fly" row, and Start; no instruments before or after a
  flight. The rotate prompt during a flight is unchanged.
- **Tablets and desktops unchanged.** The 4.4a strip applies only when the
  screen is taller than 500 px or upright; screenshots before and after are
  identical at desktop and tablet sizes.
- **Tested sizes** now model the browsers: iPhone Safari 734 × 331 and 393 × 652,
  installed 852 × 393 with 59/59/0/21 px insets; A70 Chrome about 914 × 355 and
  411 × 786, installed 914 × 411.

The HUD and the touch controls are sibling elements, so the yaw column and the
zone tops follow the HUD's card positions by shared numbers, commented on both
sides; the browser tests check the result at every size.

## Alternatives

- **Rotate the page with CSS in portrait** (draw landscape sideways): keeps
  flying possible upright but fights the OS (keyboard, notch, status bar) and
  every coordinate the touch controls compute.
- **A portrait flight layout:** possible, but thumbs would cover the scene, and
  the owner chose landscape.
- **Only `user-scalable=no`:** not enough on iOS.
- **Auto-resume after rotating back:** convenient, but the lander would move
  while the player is still re-gripping the phone.

## Consequences and revisit conditions

- Glass blur stays on by default on phones; Help's "Reduce transparency" turns it
  off. The `?debug` overlay now names the screen size and pixel ratio,
  orientation, input device, touch controls, and whether panels are glass or
  solid, so the owner can measure on device.
- iOS Safari edge gestures (home indicator, back swipe) belong to the system;
  they can cancel a touch, which the touch controls release cleanly.
- Revisit if on-device checks show the zones are too small or too large, if the
  blur costs too much on the A70, or if a portrait layout is wanted later.
