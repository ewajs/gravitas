# Landing

## What counts as a landing

The game measures the moment a leg first touches the ground, then waits for the
lander to be still.

| Result                  | When                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Landed on the pad**   | You touch down at 2 m/s or less downward, 1 m/s or less sideways, under 10° of tilt, then stay still on all four legs for a second with your centre over the pad. |
| **Landed off the pad**  | The same, but away from the target: safe, just not on the mark.                                                                                                   |
| **Too rough a landing** | You ended up standing, but the touchdown broke a speed or tilt limit.                                                                                             |
| **Crash**               | The structure hit the ground, a leg struck faster than 5 m/s, or the lander tipped over.                                                                          |

A value exactly at a limit counts as within it. If you touch and lift off again
for more than a second, the game starts measuring again.

## The legs

The legs compress like shock absorbers: they soak up a gentle touchdown and leave
the lander resting. A hard hit compresses them fully and lets the engine bell or
the structure strike the ground. Leaning or drifting, the lander can slide or tip:
past 45° on two legs, it falls over.

## Tips

- **Fuel:** the first descent carries 60 kg. A direct approach uses about 24 kg;
  hovering without descending runs it dry in about a minute and a half.
- **Fast up high, slow down low.** Near the ground, aim for under 1 m/s of descent.
- **Drift first, then height.** Use the radar to stop sideways motion before the
  last few metres.
- **Level out before touching.** 10° of tilt already makes it rough.
- **Watch the hover mark:** a little above it slows the fall; a little below lets
  it continue.
